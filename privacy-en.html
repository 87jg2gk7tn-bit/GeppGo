GeppGo helps a group organise a trip: the day plan, expenses, tickets, photos. To do that it processes data, and this page says which data, why, where it ends up and what you can ask for.

The two things that matter most

  • Photos can only be seen inside the trip they belong to. There is no public board and no address that can be passed on to strangers: the addresses used to download them last one hour and only work for people in that trip.
  • When you add a person to a trip, you are entering someone else’s data. Their name, what they spent, where they went. It is up to you to let them know: that’s the part no app can do for you.

Who processes the data

The data controller is Giacomo Merati, who develops and runs GeppGo as a private individual.
For anything at all: merati.giacomo94@gmail.com

What it collects, and why

To have an account

Your email and a password. They let you find your trips on another phone and join the trips you are invited to. Without an account GeppGo works just the same, but everything stays only on that phone: the app reminds you of this after your first trip, and at most once a week.

What you write in the trip

Names of your travel mates, stops, times, expenses and how to split them, places to stay, tickets, notes. It’s needed to make the trip work, and that is the only reason it is kept.

Some of this information concerns other people: the travel mates you add. See the box at the top.

If you import a trip from a file (Excel, Numbers, CSV) or from copied cells, the file is read on your phone and we don’t send it to anyone: it becomes a trip like the others. To put the places on the map we look up their names and addresses, just as when you search for them yourself (see the table below).

Photos

They stay on your phone and, if you have an account, they also go to the cloud so that they aren’t lost when you change phones and so that your mates in that trip can see them — only them. For each photo we keep who uploaded it, when and in which trip: this is needed to be able to follow up on a report.

For each photo the phone also makes a small copy, which goes to the cloud together with the original: it’s the one your mates download to see the strip of the day’s photos, and the full photo is only downloaded when someone actually opens it. This avoids making people on the road download hundreds of megabytes. The small copy is stored in the same place as the photo, is seen by the same people, and when you delete the photo it goes with it.

Backup

From Profile → Backup → Export data the phone makes a single file (.zip) with your trips, packing lists and the photos saved on the phone that you added. Your mates’ photos are left out, even if the phone has already downloaded them: they are theirs, and a file that gets passed around must not carry them along. The file is made on the phone and goes wherever you choose — in Files, on iCloud Drive, in a message to yourself: we don’t receive it. From then on it’s in your hands, and whoever has the file sees everything it contains: keep it where you would keep a photo album. Map tiles aren’t included: they are downloaded again.

When you restore a backup with an account, a trip shared with others never overwrites the group’s one: it comes back as a private copy that only you can see, and what your mates see stays as it is.

Packing list and settings

If you have an account, your packing list for each trip and some settings (the navigation app to open, photo quality, proximity alerts and their radius, dark theme, your name) also go to the cloud, so you find them on another phone. They are yours alone: only you can read and change them, and your travel mates, who can see the whole trip, can’t see your packing list. With each item we keep the time of its last change, which is needed to merge two phones without losing anything. Language, phone permissions and plan stay on each phone and are not sent. Deleting a trip removes the packing lists for that trip; deleting the account removes everything.

When you create your first trip, the app asks the browser to treat its data as storage not to be thrown away when the phone fills up («persistent storage»). It’s a request that stays on the phone: it doesn’t send anything to anyone.

Where you are

Your location is requested only when it’s needed — finding a toilet, a cash machine, a smoking area, calling the group with «Gather round», telling you that you are near a stop — and never when the app opens: before the phone asks you for permission, the app tells you what it needs it for. We don’t store it: it is sent to whoever has to answer, and that’s the end of it. You can refuse it: the rest of the app works, and the weather and city at the top of the home screen come from the trip’s destination, not from where you are.

One thing is kept, but only on your phone: when you look for what’s nearby, the app keeps the answer and the point you asked from for one day, so as not to ask the maps the same thing twice. It’s at most the last sixty searches, they delete themselves, and they never leave the phone: they don’t reach us or anyone else. If you clear the app’s data they disappear immediately.

«Gather round»

Whoever organises a group trip can call the others with one button: their phones receive an alert with the point where the caller is, and the way to get there. It is the only case in which a location leaves your phone and reaches other people, and it works like this:

  • it’s the location of the caller, never of those receiving it. Nobody is located: one person says where they are, the others decide whether to go there.
  • it is taken at that moment, when the button is pressed. Nothing is started that follows you over time, and the call never updates: if the point changes, a new one is made.
  • it is seen only by the people in that trip, and only a trip admin can make it.
  • it expires after two hours. After that, the database no longer shows it to anyone. The caller can withdraw it earlier, and deleting your account removes your calls with it.

If you don’t want this to happen, just don’t give the app permission to see where you are: the button warns that without a location it can’t tell the others where you are, and sends nothing.

What you ask the assistant

GeppGo’s assistant is Gemma, an open artificial intelligence model (Google made it and published it so that anyone can run it), which runs on Cloudflare Workers AI: the requests go to Cloudflare, not to Google. They don’t leave your phone directly: they go through a GeppGo bridge (a small program, also on Cloudflare), which passes them to the model and brings you back the answer. The bridge doesn’t keep what passes through it, and the app adds neither your email nor your account.

Workers AI’s terms say that Cloudflare doesn’t use what we send it — questions, photos, answers — to train models or to improve its services or anyone else’s, and that it only keeps it if the customer puts it in one of its own stores: the bridge doesn’t. Requests are processed on Cloudflare’s network, which may do so outside Europe as well.

The assistant only receives what’s needed for the request you make:

  • in the chat, what you write, the latest messages of the conversation and the plan of the open trip: destination, dates, weather, names and times of the stops, with a location rounded to about one hundred metres. Not your mates’ names, not the expenses, not where you are;
  • the photo of a place to recognise, or the screenshot of a reel: a redrawn copy, without the file’s hidden data (the GPS point where it was taken, the phone model);
  • the text you ask it to read: an itinerary pasted or in a file, the PDF of a packing list, a hotel’s confirmation email. Before sending it, the phone removes what it recognises as an email address, card number, IBAN or phone number (in the hotel email the phone numbers stay, because the place’s number is needed). It’s an automatic filter and something may slip through; people’s names may remain: in an itinerary they are used to create the trip’s travel mates;
  • the pages of a scanned PDF (a PDF made of photographs, with no text inside): the first three pages are sent as images, made smaller. From an image the phone can’t remove emails, phone numbers or other numbers as it does with text: if the PDF contains them, they are sent too;
  • for suggestions, the names of the places, the destination and the group’s tastes.

Web searches

For facts that change — whether a place needs booking and, in the chat, current opening hours, events or closures — the assistant searches the web with Tavily, a search engine made for artificial intelligence assistants. Tavily receives only a search line, made of place names, the trip’s destination, a date and the topic (for example «Torre de Belém Lisbon opening hours 2026-11-03»): never people’s names, emails, phone numbers, what you write in the chat or other personal data. The line is put together by the bridge, using only the trip’s places that are on the map and that don’t contain the name of a travel mate; an automatic test checks this at every change of the app. The search is made by the bridge: Tavily doesn’t receive your phone’s IP address.

Tavily’s terms allow it to keep the searches and the results and to use them to improve its models, also through its artificial intelligence providers. Tavily may process them outside Europe as well. If the search isn’t available or has run out of credits for the month, the assistant answers anyway with what it knows, and says so.

The stops’ opening hours and the check of the places in a pasted list, on the other hand, don’t go through the assistant: we look them up on the OpenStreetMap map (see the table below), with the place name and the city.

Don’t send sensitive data to the assistant: health, identity documents, card numbers, passwords and access codes, or things about other people that they wouldn’t want passed around. They aren’t needed to organise a trip. The assistant isn’t compulsory: the rest of the app works without it, and the details of a hotel or a stop can be typed in by hand.

Reports

If you report a photo, we keep the report, the reason, what you wrote and which photo it refers to. Reports remain even if whoever made them deletes their account, but without their name any more: a report about serious content isn’t data about the reporter, it’s the record of something that happened.

What it does NOT do

  • It doesn’t sell data to anyone.
  • It doesn’t use it for profiling or targeted advertising.
  • It doesn’t track you from one site to another, and doesn’t use profiling cookies.
  • It doesn’t receive or keep a history of where you have been: the latest searches stay on the phone for one day and don’t reach us.

On what grounds

  • To provide the service (art. 6.1.b GDPR): account, trip content, photos, packing list and settings, syncing.
  • With your consent (art. 6.1.a): location and notifications, which the phone asks you for and which you can withdraw whenever you like from the settings.
  • For a legitimate interest (art. 6.1.f): keeping the record of uploads and the reports, that is being able to respond if someone posts something they shouldn’t.

The map bridge, in detail

When you ask where the nearest toilet is, or look for a hotel, or the app needs to work out where a city is, the question no longer goes from your phone to the OpenStreetMap map: it goes through a function of ours on Supabase, which passes it to the map once for everyone and keeps the answer. Whoever looks for the same thing in the same area after you doesn’t trigger any call: they read that one. It’s needed because OpenStreetMap’s services are run by volunteers, and their rules ask that an app doesn’t call them from every phone.

This means that a location goes through a server of ours, and we tell you so clearly. However:

  • Often there is no location at all. Searching for «Colosseum, Rome» or the name of a hotel is just text: it’s the same question for anyone in the world, and indeed the first person who asks it asks it for all the others.
  • And when there is one, it isn’t yours. The phone rounds the point to a grid of about two hundred metres before sending the question, and the bridge rejects questions that arrive with a location that isn’t rounded instead of forwarding them. We receive the square, not you inside the square — and that’s also what makes your question match that of someone who lives in your street.
  • Nothing says who you are. No name, no email, no identifier of the phone or the account travels with it.
  • We don’t keep a record of who asked what. What stays in the database is the map’s answer — which is a public map — with the fingerprint of the question as a label. There is no «who» column, because that data doesn’t exist.
  • Answers are thrown away after thirty days.

If the bridge doesn’t respond, the app goes back to asking the map directly, as it did before: it doesn’t get stuck.

Google Maps links

A long Google Maps link pasted into GeppGo is read on the phone, and nothing is sent anywhere. Short ones (maps.app.goo.gl/…, the ones from the “Share” button in Google Maps) don’t contain the place: you have to open them to find out, and from inside the app the phone can’t. So the link goes through a function of ours on Supabase, which opens it at Google and returns the real address, from which the app reads the place.

  • Only the link arrives. No name, no email, no account identifier.
  • Google sees our server, not your phone: your IP address doesn’t reach Google.
  • We don’t keep it. The link and the address it leads to aren’t written down anywhere; the function’s log only keeps how it went (done, timed out, refused), never the link.
  • The function sees your IP address, like any server, and uses it only to count requests (at most thirty in ten minutes, so that nobody can use it to send traffic to Google with our face): it stays in the function’s memory for ten minutes and isn’t saved.
  • The function only opens Google Maps addresses, and stops if a redirect leads somewhere else.

Google Maps, Google Calendar and museum websites only open when you tap a button: from then on their rules apply, not ours.

Where the data is. The Supabase servers GeppGo runs on are in Stockholm, Sweden (region eu-north-1): inside the European Economic Area. Your email, trips, photos, packing list and settings stay there, and are not transferred outside Europe to be stored.

Requests to the assistant, on the other hand, are processed by Cloudflare, and web searches by Tavily: both also outside Europe, under the rules written in «What you ask the assistant» and «Web searches».

For how long

  • For as long as you keep it. A trip stays until you delete it; a photo until you remove it.
  • Deleting the account removes your email, your trip memberships, the photos you uploaded, your packing list and your settings. Trips that still have someone else in them stay with them: it isn’t fair to take away a trip that belongs to five people.
  • Reports remain, without the name of whoever made them, for the time needed to handle them and to show how they were handled.
  • On your phone the data stays until you do a reset from Profile → Backup, even after deleting the account. On iPhone, if you use GeppGo in Safari without having added it to the Home Screen, Safari may delete it by itself after seven days without opening it: that’s why the app explains how to add it.

What you can ask for

You have the right to know what data we hold, to correct it, to delete it, to restrict its use, to object to its processing and to receive it in a readable format.

  • Delete everything: Profile → Delete my account. It’s inside the app, you don’t need to ask anyone’s permission.
  • Take your data with you: Profile → Backup → Export data, which gives you a file with everything that’s on the phone, photos included.
  • For anything else write to merati.giacomo94@gmail.com.

If you think something is wrong, you can contact the Italian data protection authority, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali.

If something shouldn’t be there

Every photo has Report. Reports concerning minors or crimes are looked at first and, when needed, passed on to the authorities. If someone is in danger right now, call 112; in Italy material concerning minors can also be reported to the Postal Police, on commissariatodips.it.

Children and teenagers

GeppGo isn’t meant for anyone under 14 years old and must not be used by them without the consent of whoever is responsible for them. If we find out that an account belongs to a minor under that age, we close it.

Security

The connection is encrypted. Anyone who isn’t part of a trip can’t read its data or photos: it isn’t a promise, it’s how the database is built — the permissions are written in supabase-schema.sql, in the app’s public code, and there are automatic tests that check them at every change.

If this page changes

The date at the top says since when the version you are reading applies. If something important changes, we say so inside the app before it comes into force.
